## Context

The company had spent 9 months and ~$1.4M building an internal AI assistant for the support team. At launch, 6% of agents used it weekly. Most who tried it once never came back. Leadership was considering killing it.

I inherited the product. My mandate: 60 days to decide whether to fix it or shut it down.

## Why this was hard *because* it was AI

The previous team had built a *technically impressive* agent — multi-step reasoning, tool use, the works. It was failing for three deeply AI-shaped reasons:

1. **The agent was slower than the human.** Average response time was 14 seconds. Support agents are graded on handle time. Every second of agent latency was a tax on the user's KPI.
2. **It optimized for the wrong unit of work.** It tried to answer tickets end-to-end. What agents actually wanted was a draft they could edit in 5 seconds.
3. **No exit ramp.** When the agent was wrong, agents had to start over from scratch. The product had no "I'll take it from here" handoff.

I spent the first 2 weeks shadowing 11 support agents instead of looking at code. That's where the real problems lived.

## Key decisions & trade-offs

**Reframed from "answer the ticket" to "give me a head start."** This was the biggest decision and the hardest internal sell. Eng had built sophisticated agentic chains. I asked them to delete most of it. We shipped a single-shot draft generator in 3 weeks.

**Measured time-to-edit, not accuracy.** New north star metric. We didn't care if the draft was "right" — we cared whether the agent could make it ship-ready in under 30 seconds. This changed how we evaluated every prompt change.

**Killed the chat interface. Made it inline in the existing ticket UI.** Removed a context switch. Adoption tripled in 2 weeks just from this change.

**Built an explicit "this draft was useless" button.** Negative signal collection was more valuable than positive. Every "useless" click flagged the source ticket for the eval set.

## Outcome

- Weekly active adoption: **6% → 64%** in one quarter
- Internal NPS: **−12 → +41**
- Average handle time: **−18%** for tickets where the tool was used
- Tool was expanded to sales and onboarding teams the following quarter

## What I'd do differently

I'd have killed the chat interface in week 1, not week 4. I knew within 3 days of shadowing that it was the core problem. I delayed because I didn't want to make a big call before "earning" credibility with the team. That was a mistake — *making the right call early* is how you earn credibility.

Also: I'd write the "kill" version of the memo even when I'm leaning fix. Forcing yourself to articulate the case for shutdown sharpens the case for continuation.
