## Context

I'd been writing PRDs that referenced "LLM evals" for two years without ever having built one end-to-end myself. The gap was starting to bother me — I could specify what I wanted, but I couldn't feel the trade-offs.

One weekend I built [Tool Name]: a lightweight CLI for running pairwise human evals on LLM outputs. ~600 lines of Python.

## Why I built it

The internal eval tool at my company was great for production, terrible for exploration. I wanted something I could point at a folder of JSONL outputs and get a graded comparison in under 5 minutes. Specifically:

1. Pairwise comparison only (no Likert scales — they're noisy at small N)
2. Local-first (no SaaS, no upload — I wanted to grade sensitive outputs)
3. Inter-rater agreement built in (because every team I've worked with skipped this and regretted it)

## What I learned that changed how I PM

**Building the eval was 10× more informative than reading about evals.** The first time I saw two annotators disagree on outputs *I thought were obviously different*, I understood why our internal disagreement rates were 30% — and why our model comparisons were noisier than the team thought.

**Pairwise > absolute scoring, by a lot.** Annotators were ~3× faster and agreement was ~2× higher with "A or B" than with 1-5 scales. I have since pushed every team I work with to switch.

**The hardest part of evals is example selection, not grading.** I spent more time writing the sampling logic than the UI. PMs almost never own this and we should.

## Why this matters for an AI PM portfolio

I think the bar for senior AI PMs is shifting. It's no longer enough to *speak* the language of ML — you need to have *felt* the trade-offs at least once. Side projects are the cheapest way to do that. This one cost me a Saturday and changed how I make decisions five days a week.
