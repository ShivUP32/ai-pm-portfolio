## Context

[Company] users were spending [X hours/week] on [task]. A previous attempt to automate it with rules-based logic failed because [reason]. When GPT-4 launched, the team built a prompt-engineered prototype in a week — it demoed beautifully and broke in production within a day.

I was brought in to take it from "magic demo" to "feature people trust enough to use weekly."

## Why this was hard *because* it was AI

Three things made this an AI problem, not a normal product problem:

1. **No ground truth.** "Correct" output was subjective — two domain experts agreed only 70% of the time. We had to build the eval rubric before we could build the product.
2. **Trust decay.** One bad answer cost us ~5 sessions of user trust. Our error budget was effectively zero for high-stakes queries, even though average quality was good.
3. **Cost per query was 100× the rest of the product.** Naive scaling would have made the feature unprofitable at our pricing.

## Key decisions & trade-offs

**Chose RAG + structured output over fine-tuning.** Fine-tuning would have given us 3-5% quality lift but locked us into a model version and made debugging opaque. RAG gave us auditability — when something went wrong, we could point to the retrieved chunk.

**Built the eval harness before the v1 feature.** Spent the first 3 weeks shipping an internal eval tool, not user-facing code. Eng pushed back. I held the line. By month 2 we had 400 graded examples that became our regression suite — every prompt change ran against it in CI.

**Shipped with confidence scores visible to users.** Counterintuitive call: instead of hiding uncertainty, we surfaced it ("I'm 60% confident in this answer"). Trust scores went *up*, not down. Users felt the product was honest.

**Capped context at 6K tokens, not 32K.** Quality plateaued past 6K and cost tripled. Held this against eng requests for bigger context. Stuck.

## Outcome

- Hallucination rate (eval set): **47% → 8%** in 4 months
- Weekly active usage: **3.2× baseline** by month 6
- Cost per query: **down 71%** via caching + smaller model routing
- Eval framework adopted by 2 other AI feature teams in the company

## What I'd do differently

I'd build the user-facing feedback loop in week 1, not month 3. We had to backfill thousands of implicit signals because we shipped without thumbs up/down. Costly mistake.

I also underestimated how much PM time would go into *model selection meetings*. Senior PMs need to be in the room when applied science picks the base model — that's a product decision wearing a research costume.
